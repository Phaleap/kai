Protein: for building muscle while getting leaner, a common target is about 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kg of body weight per day, spread over 3 to 5 meals.

Calories for recomposition: a small deficit or maintenance calories works best for building muscle and losing fat at the same time. Crash diets under about 1200 calories a day are not recommended and cost muscle.

Progressive overload: to grow, gradually do more over time. Add 1 to 2 reps per set each week, and when you reach the top of your rep range, add the smallest weight increase, usually 2.5 kg.

Rep ranges: most muscle growth work is done in 6 to 15 reps per set, close to 1 to 3 reps before failure. Heavy compound lifts like squat, bench and rows can use the lower end.

Rest between sets: rest 2 to 3 minutes after heavy compound lifts and 60 to 90 seconds after smaller isolation exercises.

Weekly volume: a good starting point is 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group per week. Beginners can start at the low end.

Recovery and sleep: aim for 7 to 9 hours of sleep. Muscle grows during recovery, not during the workout. Take at least 1 full rest day, and reduce volume if you feel worn out for several days.

Running day: one easy run per week, 30 to 40 minutes at a pace where you can still talk, is good for heart health and does not hurt muscle gain. Avoid doing it right before a heavy leg day.

Warm-up: start every session with 5 minutes of light cardio, then 1 or 2 lighter warm-up sets of the first exercise before the heavy sets.

Pain: normal muscle soreness fades in 2 to 3 days. Sharp pain, joint pain or pain that keeps getting worse should be checked by a doctor or physiotherapist, and the coach must not diagnose it.
